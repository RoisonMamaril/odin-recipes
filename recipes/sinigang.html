Sinigang

Description

Sinigang is a Filipino sour soup with pork belly, tamarind broth, and vegetables like daikon radish, eggplant, string beans, and kangkong. The broth is bright and tangy from tamarind and savory from the pork fat rendered during simmering.

This pork version is the most common sinigang in Filipino households, served hot with rice and fish sauce with crushed siling labuyo on the side. The traditional boiled method keeps the broth clear while giving the vegetables a fresh bite.

Ingredients

  • Pork belly
  • Young tamarind or sinigang mix
  • Water spinach (kangkong)
  • String beans
  • Eggplant
  • Daikon radish
  • Okra
  • Tomatoes
  • Long green pepper (siling pansigang)
  • Onion
  • Fish sauce
  • Water

Steps

  1. Boil the tamarind in water for about 40 minutes, then strain the broth and squeeze the pulp to extract the sour flavor.
  2. Pour the tamarind broth into a pot, add onion, tomatoes, and the pork belly, then bring to a boil.
  3. Skim off any scum from the surface, add fish sauce, then cover and simmer until the pork is tender.
  4. Add daikon and eggplant first, then cook for a few minutes to soften them.
  5. Add long green peppers, string beans, remaining tomatoes, and okra, then continue cooking for several minutes.
  6. Add the kangkong stalks and leaves last, season with more fish sauce if needed, then turn off the heat and let the residual heat wilt the greens.
  7. Serve immediately with steamed rice and fish sauce with crushed siling labuyo on the side.
